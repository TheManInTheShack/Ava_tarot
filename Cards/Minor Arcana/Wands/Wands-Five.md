# Five of Wands

Five figures, each holding a wand, clash them together in an apparent struggle. But closer inspection reveals the struggle is stylized, almost like a dance. There is conflict here, but also energy and engagement. The Five of Wands is the friction that arises when multiple passions and wills occupy the same space.

## Upright

The Five of Wands appears when competition, disagreement, or conflict arises. This may be external—a workplace dispute, a creative collaboration that became contentious, competing interests that cannot all be served. Or it may be internal—inner conflict between different desires, ambitions pulling in different directions. The card is not inherently destructive; it is the friction that arises when passion meets passion. Sometimes the Five indicates a testing ground, where competing ideas are clashed together to find the strongest. The question is whether the conflict will sharpen you or destroy you.

## Reversed

Reversed, the conflict resolves. The wands lower; the agreement is reached. Cooperation replaces competition; opposing wills find common ground. Or the conflict was illusory and is now seen clearly as such. Sometimes reversed Five indicates a decision to stop fighting—to release the conflict rather than resolve it, to walk away from a struggle that was not truly yours.
