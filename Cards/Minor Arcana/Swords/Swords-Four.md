# Four of Swords

A figure lies in repose, at peace, while four swords hang on the wall above—the conflicts are set aside, not gone but temporarily put away. This is the moment of recovery after struggle, the necessary rest before the next battle.

## Upright

The Four of Swords is rest, respite, and the necessary pause in conflict. After the Two's stalemate and the Three's pain, the Four offers recovery. This can be literal—a rest day, a vacation, a period of healing. Or it is mental and emotional: meditation, contemplation, the deliberate setting aside of struggle in order to recover strength. The card celebrates the wisdom of rest and the necessity of pause. You cannot fight forever; recovery is not weakness but wisdom.

## Reversed

Reversed, rest is denied or rejected. Restlessness, anxiety, the inability to stop. Nervous energy that prevents genuine recovery. Or the rest was too long; you are stagnating rather than recovering. The Four reversed asks: why do you refuse to rest? What are you afraid will happen if you pause?
