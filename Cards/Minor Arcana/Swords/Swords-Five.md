# Five of Swords

A figure gathers swords from the ground while others walk away, defeated. The victorious figure wears an expression not of triumph but of hollowness. All have lost something in this conflict. This is the card of pyrrhic victory—winning but in such a way that the victory costs more than the loss.

## Upright

The Five of Swords appears when conflict leads to defeat, dishonor, and bitterness. This is not the honest loss of battle but the empty feeling that comes from victory gained through means that violated values. The card often indicates that you are holding onto a conflict that has already been lost, that the battle is over and you are still fighting the ghosts of it. The Five asks: is this fight worth what it costs? Have you already lost what you are fighting to protect?

## Reversed

Reversed, the conflict resolves, or you release your attachment to winning it. Reconciliation becomes possible; you accept the loss and move forward. Wisdom comes from understanding what the conflict cost and choosing differently next time. The sword is set down; the battle is finished.
