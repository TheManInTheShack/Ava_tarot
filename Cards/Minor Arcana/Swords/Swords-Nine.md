# Nine of Swords

A figure sits upright in bed, hands over face, in the dark hours. Nine swords hang on the wall behind them. Nothing in this image is physically happening — the swords are on the wall, not in the figure's body. The Nine of Swords depicts the mind at war with itself: the suffering is real, but it is the suffering of thought, not of circumstance.

## Upright

Anxiety, dread, and the 3am spiral. The mind rehearses disasters that have not occurred and may never occur. The Nine of Swords asks you to look clearly at what you are actually afraid of — often the fear of the thing is more consuming than the thing itself. It can also indicate genuine grief or guilt that needs to be addressed rather than suppressed.

## Reversed

The turning point. When the Nine of Swords reverses, the worst of the anxiety is passing — the figure is beginning to lower their hands from their face. Recovery is possible; the inner critic is losing its grip. Sometimes it indicates that the fears were worse than the reality, and daylight is coming.

## Elemental and Astrological

Air at its most turbulent and self-referential — thought consuming thought, the mind as its own tormentor. The Swords suit traces a difficult arc: from the clarity of the Ace to the collective grief of the Ten, with much struggle in between.
