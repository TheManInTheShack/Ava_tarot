# Seven of Cups

A silhouetted figure faces seven cups floating in clouds, each containing a different vision: a castle, a wreath of laurel, a dragon, a snake, a shrouded figure, a jewel-laden head, and a cup overflowing. All are possible; none are guaranteed. The figure is transfixed — enchanted or overwhelmed, it is hard to say which.

## Upright

The paralysis of too many possibilities. When imagination runs ahead of action and every option looks equally real and equally compelling, the Seven of Cups appears. This can be a gift — creative abundance, a moment of genuine potential — or a trap, where fantasy substitutes for commitment. The cups in the clouds are not lies, but they are not yet real. Only the choice to reach for one makes any of them manifest.

## Reversed

The clouds clear. A choice has been made, or the illusions have been recognized for what they are. Reversed Seven of Cups is often a more grounded position than upright — the fantasy is released, the real option is chosen, focus replaces enchantment.
