# Five of Cups

A figure stands in the rain, back turned to the viewer, looking at three overturned cups spilling their contents. Two cups stand upright behind them, but the figure's gaze is fixed on what is lost. Behind them, a figure approaches with a cloak—help or understanding is coming, but not yet recognized.

## Upright

The Five of Cups is loss and grief. Something precious has ended—a relationship, a cherished hope, a life circumstance that will not return. The card honors the genuine pain of loss while also hinting (in the standing cups, in the approaching figure) that recovery will eventually come. The Five of Cups asks you to feel what you feel, to grieve what must be grieved, without getting stuck in the grief. This is the necessary passage through sorrow, not the sorrow itself becoming permanent.

## Reversed

Reversed, the healing has begun. The figure begins to turn around; the approaching figure is recognized and welcomed. Forgiveness becomes possible—of others who caused the pain, or of yourself for what you could not prevent. Moving forward through grief, acceptance of what has changed, the beginning of restoration.
