# Four of Cups

A figure sits beneath a tree, arms crossed, gazing downward with an expression of sullenness or deep thought. Three cups are on the ground before them; a fourth cup hovers in the air, offered but not yet received. This is the card of introspection turned inward, of contemplation that has become stagnation.

## Upright

The Four of Cups appears when you are withdrawn, contemplative, perhaps melancholic. A period of introspection, but one that may have gone too far—you are missing what is being offered because you are turned inward. The card often indicates that something is being missed or overlooked, an opportunity passing by while you are preoccupied with inner feelings. It can also indicate genuine need for solitude and reflection, but with the warning that isolation can become apathy if extended too long. The Four asks: what are you not seeing because you are too focused on what you feel?

## Reversed

Reversed, the Four signals awakening from the withdrawn state. What was hidden becomes visible; the offered cup is accepted. A new beginning is possible; apathy gives way to engagement. Movement returns after a period of stillness. Sometimes reversed indicates that the period of introspection has yielded wisdom that is now ready to be expressed.
