# The Devil

A horned figure sits upon a throne, appearing more primal than evil—a shadow self, the animal nature we carry. Below the figure, two bound figures are loosely chained, their chains slack enough to slip free. The Devil is not external torment; it is the comfortable cage we build around ourselves, the habits and attachments we mistake for safety.

## Upright

The Devil appears when we are caught in patterns of our own making—addiction, codependency, unhealthy attachments, or the seductive pull of material excess. This is not a card of external evil but of self-imposed limitation. The chains are loose; freedom is always one choice away. The Devil asks: what part of yourself have you bound? What attachment masquerades as necessity? Sometimes the Devil is simply honest sexuality and embodied pleasure—reminders that the material world is not shameful, only that we must not be enslaved by it.

## Reversed

The chains break. Reversed, the Devil signals liberation from what held you bound—the moment of choosing freedom over comfort, the decision to name the pattern and refuse it. Release from addiction, the end of a toxic relationship, the reclamation of power given away. Transcendence becomes possible when the cage is recognized for what it is.

## Elemental and Astrological

Capricorn/Saturn: the earthiness of material reality and the weight of consequence. Saturn teaches through limitation, and the Devil is Saturn's shadow—the lesson that comes from recognizing where we limit ourselves needlessly. Capricorn's pragmatism can become cynicism; the Devil is the cost of refusing to believe in anything beyond the tangible.
