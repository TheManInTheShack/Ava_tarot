# The High Priestess

The High Priestess sits between two pillars — one black (Boaz), one white (Jachin) — the same pillars that flanked the entrance to Solomon's Temple. Behind her, a veil embroidered with pomegranates hangs between the pillars; beyond it, the sea. On her lap rests a half-visible scroll. She wears a crown of the full moon flanked by crescents. Numbered **II**, she is the second principle: where the Magician was the explicit, she is the implicit.

## The Veil and What Lies Behind It

The veil is the central image. The High Priestess guards the threshold between the known and the unknown, between the conscious and the unconscious. The pomegranates — echoing Persephone's descent — mark the territory of the psyche below waking awareness. She does not draw the veil aside; she sits before it, embodying the act of holding space for what cannot yet be named.

## Upright

The High Priestess upright invites stillness. The answer you are seeking is not in the outer world — it is in the quiet you have been avoiding. Trust the intuition that has no argument to back it up. This is a card of waiting, listening, and allowing the unconscious to surface in dreams, symbols, and unguarded moments.

In practical matters, the High Priestess may signal that full information is not yet available. What you see is not the whole picture. Patience.

## Reversed

Reversed, the Priestess can indicate that inner knowing is being ignored in favor of external noise, or that someone is deliberately withholding information. Alternatively, the reversal can suggest withdrawal to the point of isolation — not the productive solitude of inner work but a retreat from necessary engagement.

## Elemental and Astrological

The High Priestess belongs to **Water** and the **Moon** — the archetype of the cyclic, the tidal, the reflective in esoteric traditions. The Moon rules Cancer and is exalted in Taurus; it governs the emotional body, memory, and the unconscious structures that shape waking life without being seen.
