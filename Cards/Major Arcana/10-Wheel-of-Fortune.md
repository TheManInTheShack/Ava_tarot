# Wheel of Fortune

A great wheel turns in the heavens, attended by creatures at each quarter — a sphinx at the top, serpents descending, figures ascending. Hebrew letters and alchemical symbols mark the rim. No one is on the wheel by merit; all are on it by virtue of being alive. What rises must fall; what falls will rise again.

## Upright

A turning point — often fortunate, always significant. The Wheel breaks stasis. A cycle completes, a new one begins, an unexpected opportunity or shift arrives. The card does not promise only good luck; it promises change, and the awareness that change is woven into the fabric of existence. The querent may not control the Wheel, but they can position themselves wisely as it turns.

## Reversed

Resistance to the inevitable turn. Clinging to a phase that has already ended, or bad luck that feels like more than chance — a run of circumstances where the Wheel seems to be turning against rather than for. Reversed Fortune invites examination of patterns: what cycles keep repeating, and why?

## Elemental and Astrological

Jupiter expands whatever it touches — here, the scope of fate itself. Fire: the Wheel moves, burns, transforms. Unlike the cautious Earth cards that follow in the suit, the Wheel's Fire is cosmological and impersonal.
