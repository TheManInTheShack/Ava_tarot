# Death

A skeleton in black armor rides a white horse. Before it, figures of every station — a king already fallen, a bishop in prayer, a child, a maiden — are arranged in various stages of acceptance or denial. A flag bears a white rose: purity of transition. In the distance, the sun rises between two towers. Death is not the end of the story.

## Upright

Profound transformation through inevitable ending. The Death card almost never signifies literal death; it signifies the death of a phase, a relationship, an identity, a way of being that has run its course. What is ending cannot be preserved — the armor of Death is impervious to argument, nostalgia, or bargaining. But the white rose and the rising sun promise that something follows. This is the card of metamorphosis, not annihilation.

## Reversed

Clinging to what is already over. The transformation is necessary but the querent resists it — staying in the dead relationship, holding the obsolete identity, refusing to grieve what must be grieved. Stagnation sets in where flow should be. Reversed Death can also indicate a transition that is moving too slowly, dragging out an ending that needs to be clean.

## Elemental and Astrological

Scorpio/Pluto: the sign and planet of underworld transformation, depth, and what is hidden beneath the surface. Water governs the emotional reality of change — Death is not a dry fact but a felt passage.
